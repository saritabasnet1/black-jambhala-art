Welcome to the Black Jambhala Art Journal, a journal devoted to one of the most striking figures in Himalayan painting: Black Jambhala, the wrathful wealth deity, dark blue-black in colour, standing amid flames, a skull cup in one hand and a jewel-spitting mongoose in the other. The journal is written for readers who want to understand the painting, not only to admire it. Each article takes one part of the subject and explains it carefully, in plain language, so that a reader who starts knowing nothing can finish knowing how to read a Black Jambhala thangka.

Black Jambhala is the wrathful form of the wealth deity in Vajrayana Buddhism. He belongs to a family of five Jambhalas — Yellow, White, Red, Green and Black — and he is the one painted for moments that cannot wait. Tantric lineages regard him as especially swift for practitioners facing debt or sudden hardship, and his fierce appearance is the image of the force needed to destroy the inner obstacles of stinginess and greed. The painting is therefore both a work of art and a teaching, and this journal treats it as both.

The journal is organised in five articles. The first explains the place of the black form among the five Jambhalas, and what makes him different from his four companions. The second is a close reading of the iconography — the dark body, the flames, the skull cup, the mongoose, the sword and the trident, and what each detail means. The third explains the practice that surrounds the deity: mantra, visualisation, offerings and empowerment, in general educational terms. The fourth follows how a thangka is painted, from the preparation of the canvas to the gold work and the brocade mounting. The fifth is a collector's guide, covering size, price, handmade versus print, hanging, framing and care.

The journal is written for collectors, students of Buddhist art, and anyone who has seen a Black Jambhala painting and wanted to understand it. No previous knowledge is assumed. The About page explains the journal's purpose in more detail, and the Blog page describes the guidance the journal offers. The Blog page lists all five articles in one place.
