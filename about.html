About — Black Jambhala Art Journal

About this journal: a plain-language guide to Traditional Black Jambhala Art, written for collectors and students of Himalayan Buddhist painting.

--- ---

The Black Jambhala Art Journal is a small, independent journal about a single subject: the traditional painting of Black Jambhala, the wrathful wealth deity of Vajrayana Buddhism. It exists because the painting deserves a careful reading, and because most writing about it is either too brief to be useful or too technical to be read by someone who is not already a specialist. This journal aims for the middle ground: accurate, plainly written, and useful to a reader who is meeting the subject for the first time.

Black Jambhala is a figure who is easy to misunderstand. He is dark blue-black, ringed with flames, holding a skull cup and a mongoose that spits jewels. To a first-time viewer he can look alarming, and the alarm is often where the looking stops. The journal's purpose is to carry the reader past that first impression, into the teaching the painting carries: that the fierceness is aimed at the inner obstacles of stinginess and greed, that the cup holds the nourishment that makes generosity possible, and that the mongoose showers wealth outward to relieve poverty. The painting is a teaching, and the journal is a guide to reading it.

The journal covers five subjects. The first article explains the place of the black form among the five Jambhalas. The second reads the iconography detail by detail. The third describes the practice in general educational terms. The fourth follows the making of a thangka in the workshop. The fifth is a collector's guide for buying, hanging and caring for a painting.

A word about limits. The journal describes the practice around Black Jambhala. It does not transmit it. The tantric practice traditionally requires empowerment from a qualified lama, and the journal points readers toward a teacher rather than offering instructions. The journal also makes no claims it cannot support: it does not invent history, awards or testimonials, and it does not promise results. What it offers is careful description, based on the painting itself and on the teaching the painting carries, written so that a reader can check it against any Black Jambhala thangka.
