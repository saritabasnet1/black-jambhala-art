The Black Jambhala Art Journal welcomes questions and suggestions from readers. If you have a question about a painting, about the articles on this site, or about what the journal should cover next, you are welcome to write.

To get in touch, please use the form below and enter your own details:

  • Full Name
  • Email
  • Subject (optional)
  • Message

Please write your message in full, so that it can be answered properly. Questions about reading a thangka, about collecting, or about the practice described in the articles are all welcome. The journal answers questions about the subject of the site — the traditional painting of Black Jambhala and the teaching it carries — and it does not offer practice instructions, which are received from a qualified lama.

When you write, it helps to say a little about where you are starting from. A reader who has just bought a first painting will have different questions from a reader who has collected for years, and a reader who is studying Buddhist art will have different questions from a reader who simply saw a painting and wanted to understand it. There is no wrong starting point. The journal is written for beginners as well as for collectors, and every question is read with the same attention.

A few subjects come up often, and they are worth mentioning here. Readers often ask how to tell a handmade thangka from a print, how to judge the quality of the gold work, and where a painting should be hung. Those questions are answered in the collector's guide. Readers also ask about the meaning of the skull cup and the mongoose, and about the difference between the black form and the other four Jambhalas. Those questions are answered in the article on reading the iconography and the article on the five Jambhalas. Questions about the practice itself are answered in general educational terms in the article on Black Jambhala in practice.

For readers who are new to the subject, the best place to start is the Home page and the Blog page, which lists all five articles. The About page explains the journal's purpose, and the Blog page describes the guidance the journal offers. Messages are read by the journal's editor, and replies are sent to the email address given in the form.
